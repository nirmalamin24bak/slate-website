Upma calories

By Nirmal Amin. Checked on 14 October 2026.

A plate of rava upma from 40 g of semolina has about 310 calories, and a 200 ml katori about 215. With a cup of chai it's about 375. Ghee matters as much as the rava: a teaspoon on top takes the plate to about 355. Udupi upma, a bigger plate with ghee, runs about 310 to 510.

Upma is roasted semolina (rava, suji) cooked in water with a tempering of mustard seeds, urad dal, curry leaves, green chilli, ginger and onion. It's a quick breakfast across south and west India, made plain or with peas, carrot and beans, and in Karnataka it's uppittu or kharabath.

Semolina and oil make up most of upma's calories, in about equal parts in this textbook recipe. How much water it's cooked with changes what a katori holds.

Typical values for a home kitchen, for the portion named next to each number. Other sites often show bigger numbers because they count a bigger serving or a restaurant recipe. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

What changes the calories in upma?

  • Oil or ghee matters as much as the rava. Each teaspoon adds about 40 to 45 calories.
  • Vegetables like peas, carrot and beans add volume and a few calories, so a vegetable upma has less per katori.
  • Cashews in the tempering add about 60 calories per 10 g.
  • Wetter upma fills more katoris with the same calories. Count by the rava when you can.

How were these numbers worked out?

The recipe is the semolina upma in Basic Food Preparation (via the Indian Nutrient Databank): 40 g of coarse semolina with 5 g of urad dal, 30 g of onion, green chilli, ginger, curry leaves, mustard seeds and lemon, cooked in 15 g of oil and 100 ml of water, for 2 small bowls, the oil counted as eaten, at IFCT 2017 values. The ingredients added together weigh about 208 g, so the plate, the whole recipe, is 310 calories and the pot 150 per 100 g; a katori is measured at USDA's 170 g per cup for upma, so a 200 ml katori is 215. The checks pull apart, ICMR-NIN's cup of upma at 270 calories 25% above and USDA's survey upma at 87 per 100 g 42% below, because the water content differs, so the page shows a range. The restaurant range assumes a plate up to 1.5 times as big with up to a teaspoon of ghee on top. Every dish on Slate is worked out the same way: how Slate counts calories.
