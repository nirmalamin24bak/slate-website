Chicken biryani calories

Checked on 13 October 2026.

A home plate of chicken biryani (50 g raw rice and 50 g chicken, about 305 g cooked) has about 385 calories. A restaurant plate is often 425 to 815.

Chicken biryani is rice parboiled with whole spices and layered with chicken marinated in curd and spices, fried onion and saffron milk, then sealed and cooked on dum. Hyderabadi, Lucknowi, Kolkata and Malabar biryanis each have their own style, and it's the most ordered dish on Indian delivery apps.

Rice is close to half the calories in a home plate. Restaurant biryani usually has much more ghee or oil, which is why it comes out higher.

Typical values for a home kitchen, for the portion named next to each number. Other sites often show bigger numbers because they count a bigger serving or a restaurant recipe. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

How does home chicken biryani compare with outside?

Restaurant and delivery biryani is usually made with a lot more ghee or oil than a home or textbook version, and portions are often bigger.

No lab has measured an Indian restaurant biryani, so the range below allows 1.5 to 3 times this recipe's fat, in a plate up to 1.5 times as big.

What changes the calories in chicken biryani?

  • Ghee or oil is the biggest variable. Each tablespoon across the pot adds about 120 calories, about 60 to each plate.
  • More chicken adds protein and some fat. Leg pieces and chicken with skin have more fat than breast.
  • Fried onions take up oil, and a boiled egg adds about 70 calories.
  • Raita and mirchi ka salan are separate dishes.

How were these numbers worked out?

No standard textbook in the Indian Nutrient Databank has a chicken biryani, so this follows The Art & Science of Cooking's mutton biryani, with 100 g of boneless chicken in place of the mutton: 100 g rice, onion, tomato, ginger, garlic, curd, saffron milk and spices, for 2 plates. The databank records the fat as 4 teaspoons. Values are from IFCT 2017.

The rice is taken at 2.98 times its raw weight once cooked, and katoris use USDA's 196 g per cup for biryani with chicken. IFCT 2017's chicken breast has more fat than USDA's.

That works out to about 125 calories per 100 g of cooked chicken biryani. Calories are rounded to the nearest 5 and grams to the nearest whole number.

USDA's survey values for biryani run from about 104 calories per 100 g for chicken biryani to 145 for biryani with meat, either side of this page's 125. Home chicken biryani usually falls in that span.
