Chakli calories

By Nirmal Amin. Checked on 15 October 2026.

One chakli of about 18 g, a spiral of rice flour and roasted gram dal fried in oil, has about 90 calories; 2 about 180 and 4 about 365. The oil taken up in frying is a third of the number. Packed chakli, by its labels, runs about 85 to 140 a piece, bigger Diwali chaklis at the top.

Chakli is a stiff dough of rice flour and roasted gram dal flour, with butter, sesame and chilli, pressed through a star nozzle into spirals and deep-fried until crisp. Murukku in Tamil Nadu and chakkuli in Karnataka are made the same way, sometimes with urad dal flour in place of the gram dal. It keeps for weeks, which is why it fills Diwali faral boxes.

Crisp fried snacks are light in water and heavy in oil, so per 100 g chakli sits well above most sweets. A single chakli is small, though; a handful is not.

Typical values for a home kitchen, for the portion named next to each number. Other sites often show bigger numbers because they count a bigger serving or a restaurant recipe. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

How does home chakli compare with outside?

Packed chakli labels run from 474 to 549 calories per 100 g, either side of this page's 490; one label with more oil lists 560. Shop chaklis for Diwali are often bigger than the 18 g one here, up to 25 g.

What changes the calories in chakli?

  • Oil taken up in frying is about a third of the number; a thicker spiral takes up less per gram, a thin crisp one more.
  • Butter in the dough is about 6% of the calories here. Butter chakli recipes with twice the butter add about 6 calories a piece.
  • Size: Diwali chaklis of 25 g or more are half as much again as this 18 g one.
  • Urad dal flour in place of the gram dal, as in murukku, changes almost nothing per gram.
  • Mathri and namak para are richer per 100 g, about 545 and 520, because their dough carries fat before frying.

How were these numbers worked out?

The Indian Nutrient Databank's standard textbooks have no chakli; its open-source recipes include a rice murukku (OSR113) whose dough is the one chakli is made from: 3 cups of rice flour, ¾ cup of roasted gram dal flour, 2 tablespoons each of butter and sesame, chilli, ajwain and salt, fried, making 35 pieces. That recipe is used here, with cups converted at USDA's weights (rice flour 120 g, roasted gram dal 150 g for ¾ cup as the databank lists). Values are from IFCT 2017, with rice flour and butter from USDA.

The databank counts all 2 cups of frying oil as eaten. This page takes the oil taken up as about 14% of the raw weight, the uptake worked out for the samosa on this site, about 3.4 g per chakli, and the finished chakli at 4% moisture as a crisp snack. Both are stated assumptions; the result, 25% fat, sits inside the 21 to 33% on packed labels and in published chakli studies, and within 12% of three labels' calories.

Questions people ask

How many calories are in 1 chakli?

About 90 for an 18 g chakli, and about 365 for four.

How many calories are in 100 g of chakli?

About 490. Packed labels list 474 to 549 per 100 g.

Is chakli the same as murukku?

The same dough family: rice flour with roasted gram dal or urad dal flour, butter and sesame, pressed through a nozzle and fried. The shapes and the dal differ by region; the calories per gram barely do.

How much fat is in chakli?

About 25 g per 100 g of this recipe, most of it frying oil, and about 5 g in one chakli.

How many calories are in a bowl of chakli?

About 245 for a small bowl of about 50 g, 2 to 3 chaklis.
