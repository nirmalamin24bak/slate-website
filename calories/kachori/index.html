Kachori calories

A small home khasta kachori has about 120 calories. Street kachoris, which a lab study found bigger and much oilier, are about 260 to 320 each.

Khasta kachori is a flaky maida pastry, rubbed with plenty of oil or ghee, filled with spiced urad or moong dal and deep-fried slowly until it puffs and crisps. Matar kachori is filled with peas, and Rajasthani pyaz kachori with onion. In north India it's a breakfast with aloo sabzi.

Even before frying, oil in the dough and filling is about a third of a home kachori's calories, and after frying it's close to half. Street kachoris hold much more.

Typical values for a home kitchen. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

Home or outside

A peer-reviewed study bought kachoris from vendors in Delhi and a nearby village and tested them in a lab. They weighed 54 to 56 g, about one and a half times this home kachori, and held 32% and 47% fat.

The range below uses those two measured kachoris. Oil used again and again, and a thick, oil-rich crust, are the usual reasons street kachoris hold so much.

One kachori. The range is the two street kachoris measured by Gupta et al.: 56 g with 17.8 g fat, and 54 g with 25.1 g fat.

What changes the number

  • Size and frying matter most. A street kachori can be one and a half times this one, with two to three times the fat.
  • The oil or ghee rubbed into the dough (moyan) makes it flaky and adds about 40 to 45 calories per teaspoon to the batch.
  • Matar and pyaz kachori fillings are a little lighter than dal; raj kachori, filled with curd and chutneys, is much more.
  • Aloo sabzi with it is a separate dish: see aloo sabzi.

How these numbers were worked out

The recipe is the khasta kachori in The Art & Science of Cooking, a standard Indian cookery textbook (via the Indian Nutrient Databank): 100 g maida rubbed with 1½ tablespoons of oil, filled with 30 g of spiced urad dal cooked in 2 teaspoons of oil, making 7 kachoris. Values are from IFCT 2017.

The databank counts all the frying oil as eaten. This page assumes a fresh home kachori ends up about 18% fat, the oil content measured in lab-fried samosa pastry (18.3%), which works out to about 2 g of oil taken up per kachori.

ICMR-NIN counts 2 pea kachoris at 380 calories, 190 each, between this small home kachori and the street ones measured in Delhi. Depending on size and oil, a kachori is anywhere from about 120 to 320 calories.

Questions people ask

How many calories are in 1 kachori?

About 120 for a small home khasta kachori, and about 260 to 320 for a street kachori.

How many calories are in kachori with aloo sabzi?

About 415 for 2 home kachoris with a 200 ml katori of aloo sabzi.

How much fat is in a kachori?

About 6 g in this home kachori. Street kachoris tested in a lab held 17.8 g and 25.1 g.

Which has more calories, kachori or samosa?

A home samosa, about 165, is bigger than this small khasta kachori at about 120. Street versions of both are much higher.
