Makhana calories

A cup of plain makhana (about 32 g) has about 105 calories. 30 g roasted in a teaspoon of ghee comes to about 145.

Makhana, or phool makhana, is the popped seed of the fox nut, a water lily grown mostly in Bihar's ponds and wetlands. It's eaten roasted with a little ghee and salt as a snack, in kheer, and in curries, and it's a common fasting-day food.

Makhana is very light by volume: a full cup weighs only about 32 g. Per gram it's a dry starch, about the same as rice, and roasting it in ghee adds to it.

Typical values for a home kitchen. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

Home or outside

Packaged and shop-roasted makhana is usually roasted in more oil, with flavourings such as peri peri, cheese or pudina, and the label often lists more fat than home-roasted makhana.

The range below allows 1.5 to 3 times the fat of a teaspoon of ghee on 30 g. Your packet's label will be more exact.

30 g. The outside range adds 2.5 to 10 g more oil, so 1.5 to 3 times the fat of a teaspoon of ghee.

What changes the number

  • Ghee or oil for roasting adds about 45 calories a teaspoon.
  • Makhana is light by volume, so a big bowl can still be under 100 calories. Weigh it once to see what your bowl holds.
  • Makhana kheer adds milk and sugar: count those too.
  • Per gram, makhana is close to rice and wheat: USDA puts it at 332 calories per 100 g.

How these numbers were worked out

Values are USDA's for dried lotus seeds, the closest analysed match, since IFCT 2017 has no makhana: 332 calories, 15.4 g of protein and 2 g of fat per 100 g. USDA lists a cup at 32 g.

The ghee row adds a teaspoon (5 g) of ghee, from IFCT 2017, to 30 g of makhana. The katori row is an assumption of 15 g for a loosely filled 150 ml katori, half of USDA's cup by weight.
