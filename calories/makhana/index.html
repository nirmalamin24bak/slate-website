Makhana calories

By Nirmal Amin. Checked on 14 October 2026.

A cup of plain makhana, about 32 g, has about 105 calories, and a small 15 g katori about 50. Roasted in a teaspoon of ghee, 30 g comes to about 145. Ghee or oil for roasting adds about 45 calories a teaspoon. Packaged or shop-roasted makhana, with more oil, runs about 165 to 230 per 30 g.

Makhana, or phool makhana, is the popped seed of the fox nut, a water lily grown mostly in Bihar's ponds and wetlands. It's eaten roasted with a little ghee and salt as a snack, in kheer, and in curries, and it's a common fasting-day food.

Makhana is very light by volume: a full cup weighs only about 32 g. Per gram it's a dry starch, about the same as rice, and roasting it in ghee adds to it.

Typical values for a home kitchen, for the portion named next to each number. Other sites often show bigger numbers because they count a bigger serving or a restaurant recipe. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

How does home makhana compare with outside?

Packaged and shop-roasted makhana is usually roasted in more oil, with flavourings such as peri peri, cheese or pudina, and the label often lists more fat than home-roasted makhana.

The range below allows 1.5 to 3 times the fat of a teaspoon of ghee on 30 g. Your packet's label will be more exact.

What changes the calories in makhana?

  • Ghee or oil for roasting adds about 45 calories a teaspoon.
  • Makhana is light by volume, so a big bowl can still be under 100 calories. Weigh it once to see what your bowl holds.
  • Makhana kheer adds milk and sugar: count those too.
  • Per gram, makhana is close to rice and wheat: USDA puts it at 332 calories per 100 g.

How were these numbers worked out?

Makhana has no entry in IFCT 2017, so Slate uses USDA's values for dried lotus seeds, the closest analysed match: 332 calories, 15.4 g of protein and 2 g of fat per 100 g, with no oil in the plain rows. USDA lists a cup at 32 g, which is the headline portion, 105 calories; the katori row is an assumption of 15 g for a loosely filled 150 ml katori, half the cup by weight. The ghee row adds a teaspoon (5 g) of ghee from IFCT 2017 to 30 g of makhana. No second analysed source for makhana exists in IFCT 2017, NIN or USDA FNDDS, so there is no cross-check. Packaged flavoured makhana is roasted in oil with seasoning, so the packet range assumes 1.5 to 3 times the fat of the ghee row; the packet's own label is the better guide.
