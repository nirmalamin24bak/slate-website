Mathri calories

By Nirmal Amin. Checked on 15 October 2026.

One home mathri of about 21 g, a flaky disc of maida with oil and ajwain, deep-fried, has about 115 calories, and 2 about 230. The oil in the dough and the oil taken up in frying are together more than half the number, which is why mathri is richer per gram than most sweets: about 545 per 100 g.

Mathri is maida kneaded with a generous moyan of oil or ghee, peppercorns and ajwain, pressed into small discs, pricked and fried slowly until flaky and pale. It is a north Indian tea-time and Diwali snack, eaten plain or with achaar, and keeps for weeks.

The textbook recipe uses 2 tablespoons of oil in the dough for 100 g of flour before any frying, which is what makes it flaky and what makes it one of the richest things on this site per gram.

Typical values for a home kitchen, for the portion named next to each number. Other sites often show bigger numbers because they count a bigger serving or a restaurant recipe. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

What changes the calories in mathri?

  • The moyan: recipes run from 1 to 4 tablespoons of oil or ghee per 100 g of maida, about 15 calories a mathri each way.
  • Oil taken up in frying is about a quarter of the number. Slow frying at low heat, which mathri needs, takes up a little more than fast frying.
  • Size: shop mathris of 30 to 40 g are half as much again to twice this one.
  • Methi mathri, with dried fenugreek leaves, and atta mathri are close per gram. A baked mathri skips the frying oil, about 30 calories less a piece from this recipe.
  • Namak para is the same dough with less fat in it, about 520 per 100 g; khasta kachori is about 120 a piece.

How were these numbers worked out?

The recipe is the mathri in The Art & Science of Cooking, a standard Indian cookery textbook (via the Indian Nutrient Databank): 100 g of maida with 2 tablespoons of oil, 8 peppercorns, ajwain and salt, kneaded, cut into discs and deep-fried, making 7 mathris. Values are from IFCT 2017. The ajwain and salt, under 4 g, are left out.

The databank counts all 480 ml of frying oil as eaten, which is far too high. This page takes the oil taken up as about 14% of the raw weight, the uptake worked out for the samosa on this site, about 3.3 g per mathri, and the finished mathri at 4% moisture as a crisp snack. Both are stated assumptions. No lab analysis of mathri was found; the result, 35% fat, is what packed mathri labels list, though those listings were not opened for this page and are not cited.

Questions people ask

How many calories are in 1 mathri?

About 115 for a home mathri of about 21 g. A big shop mathri of 30 to 40 g is half as much again to twice that.

How many calories are in 2 mathris?

About 230 for two home mathris, and about 455 for four.

How many calories are in 100 g of mathri?

About 545 for this recipe, with about 35 g of fat.

How much fat is in a mathri?

About 7 g in one mathri: the 2 tablespoons of oil in the dough and the oil taken up in frying, in roughly equal shares.

Which has more calories, mathri or samosa?

Per piece the samosa, about 165 against 115 for a mathri, because it is three times the weight. Per 100 g the mathri, which is dry and fried, has more.
