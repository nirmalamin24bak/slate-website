Dal makhani calories

A 200 ml katori of home dal makhani has about 200 calories. A restaurant katori, finished with butter and cream, is more like 265 to 335.

Dal makhani is whole black urad with a little rajma, simmered for hours with tomato, ginger and garlic, then finished with butter and cream. It was made famous by Moti Mahal in Delhi and is on almost every north Indian restaurant menu, often as the richest dal on it.

At home it's usually made with less butter and cream than in restaurants. The dal and beans give it body; the fat decides how far the number climbs.

Typical values for a home kitchen. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

Home or outside

Restaurant dal makhani is finished with a lot more butter and cream than most home versions, often with a swirl of cream on top as it's served.

No lab has measured a restaurant katori, so the range below adds 1 to 2 teaspoons of butter and 1 to 2 tablespoons of cream to this one.

What changes the number

  • Butter and cream are the biggest variables. A teaspoon of butter adds about 35 calories, and a tablespoon of cream about 30.
  • Long simmering thickens the dal. The same pot boiled down further has more in each katori.
  • Dal makhani without cream or butter, tempered with a little oil, is close to a thick rajma per katori.
  • Rice or naan with it are counted separately.

How these numbers were worked out

The recipe is the Moti Mahal dal in The Art & Science of Cooking, a standard Indian cookery textbook (via the Indian Nutrient Databank): 30 g whole urad and 10 g rajma with tomato, onion, ginger, garlic, curd, a teaspoon of cream and a tablespoon of oil. Values are from IFCT 2017, with curd and cream from USDA.

The textbook adds 1.5 cups of water and simmers it down. The databank records half a cup left in the dish, and this page uses that for the cooked weight.

That works out to about 100 calories per 100 g of cooked dal makhani. Calories are rounded to the nearest 5 and grams to the nearest whole number.

The databank's open-source dal makhani, with more water, works out to about 74 calories per 100 g, against about 100 here. Thickness and the amount of butter and cream vary a lot, so a home katori can be anywhere from about 150 to 200.

Questions people ask

How many calories are in 1 katori of dal makhani?

About 200 for a 200 ml katori made at home, and about 265 to 335 in a restaurant, depending on the butter and cream.

How many calories are in dal makhani without cream?

This home recipe has only a teaspoon of cream in the whole bowl, about 10 calories. Taking it out changes little; leaving out butter on top matters more.

How much protein is in dal makhani?

About 7 g in a 200 ml katori, from the urad and rajma.

How many calories are in 100 g of dal makhani?

About 100 for this home version. Restaurant dal makhani with more butter and cream is higher.

Which has more calories, dal makhani or dal tadka?

Dal makhani, usually by a lot. A 200 ml katori here is about 200 against about 115 for a thin dal tadka.
