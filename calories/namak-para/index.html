Namak para calories

By Nirmal Amin. Checked on 15 October 2026.

Ten home namak para, about 50 g, have about 260 calories; one 5 g diamond about 25, and 100 g about 520. The ghee in the dough and the oil taken up in frying are together about half the number. Packed namak para, by its labels, runs about 230 to 305 for 50 g.

Namak para is maida kneaded with ghee, ajwain and salt, rolled out, cut into diamonds and deep-fried until crisp and pale gold. It is the savoury half of a Diwali plate, next to shakarpara, and the same dough makes mathri when it is pressed into discs.

A single piece is a mouthful. Ten of them, a small bowl, are what people eat with chai, and that is the portion this page leads with.

Typical values for a home kitchen, for the portion named next to each number. Other sites often show bigger numbers because they count a bigger serving or a restaurant recipe. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

How does home namak para compare with outside?

Packed namak para labels run from 459 to 607 calories per 100 g, around this page's 520. The spread is oil: the oiliest label lists 43 g of fat per 100 g against 29 g here. Pieces in a packet are often smaller than a home diamond, so count by weight.

What changes the calories in namak para?

  • Oil taken up in frying is about a quarter of the number. Thin, well-drained pieces take up less; re-used oil and thicker pieces more.
  • The ghee moyan in the dough is another fifth. Recipes run from 1 to 4 tablespoons per 2 cups of maida, about 25 calories a piece either way.
  • Baked namak para skips the frying oil, about 6 calories less a piece from this recipe.
  • Atta in place of maida adds fibre and changes the calories very little.
  • Mathri is the same dough with more fat in it, about 115 a piece; shakarpara is the sweet version, about 300 for 10.

How were these numbers worked out?

The Indian Nutrient Databank's standard textbooks have a mathri and two fortified namak para recipes (soyabean, and spinach and peanut), but no plain namak para, so the recipe is Hebbar's Kitchen's namak pare: 2 cups of maida with 3 tablespoons of ghee, ajwain, kalonji and salt, kneaded stiff, cut into diamonds and deep-fried. Cups and tablespoons are converted at USDA's weights (flour 120 g a cup, ghee 12.8 g a tablespoon). Values are from IFCT 2017. The kalonji, ajwain and salt, about 10 g together, are left out of the arithmetic.

The oil taken up is set at about 14% of the raw weight, the uptake worked out for the samosa on this site, and the finished pieces at 4% moisture as a crisp snack. Both are stated assumptions; the result, 29% fat, sits inside the 25 to 43 g on packed labels, and within 12% of two of the three labels' calories.

Questions people ask

How many calories are in namak para?

About 25 for one 5 g piece, 260 for 10 pieces (50 g) and 520 per 100 g, for home namak para fried in oil.

How many calories are in 100 g of namak para?

About 520 for this recipe. Packed labels list 459 to 607 per 100 g, depending on the oil.

Is namak para the same as mathri?

The same dough, cut differently. Mathri's textbook recipe carries more fat in the dough, so it is a little richer per gram: about 545 per 100 g against 520 here.

How much fat is in namak para?

About 29 g per 100 g, half from the ghee in the dough and half from the frying oil. That is about 15 g in 10 pieces.

How many calories are in a bowl of namak para?

About 260 for a small bowl of 10 pieces, 50 g. A full katori heaped with namak para is more like 80 g, about 400.
