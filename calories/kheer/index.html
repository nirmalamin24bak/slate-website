Kheer calories

By Nirmal Amin. Checked on 15 October 2026.

A 200 ml katori of home rice kheer, made with whole milk and 1½ tablespoons of sugar, has about 225 calories. A small 150 ml katori is about 170, a big 250 ml one about 280. Milk is more than half of it, sugar a quarter. A sweet-shop kheer, thicker and sweeter, runs about 225 to 300 a katori.

Kheer is rice simmered in milk until the milk thickens, sweetened with sugar and finished with cardamom, raisins and a few slivers of almond. It is the sweet of pujas and festival thalis across north India, and payasam, doodh pak and phirni are its cousins.

The number is set by the milk and the sugar, not the rice: a katori holds only a spoonful of rice. How far the milk is simmered down changes how much milk ends up in each katori.

Typical values for a home kitchen, for the portion named next to each number. Other sites often show bigger numbers because they count a bigger serving or a restaurant recipe. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

How does home kheer compare with outside?

Sweet-shop and wedding kheer is usually simmered down further, sweeter, and sometimes made with buffalo milk, khoa or condensed milk. USDA's firni, a thicker Indian milk pudding, has a third more calories per gram than its rice pudding.

What changes the calories in kheer?

  • Sugar is about a quarter of the number. Each tablespoon in the pot adds about 50 calories, spread over the bowls.
  • How far the milk is simmered down: thicker kheer puts more milk, and more calories, into the same katori.
  • Buffalo milk raises it by about half again on the milk's share; toned milk lowers it.
  • Khoa, condensed milk or cream stirred in at the end adds a lot: a tablespoon of condensed milk is about 60 calories.
  • Seviyan kheer and sabudana kheer are close to this per katori; gajar halwa is richer, about 145 for a small katori.

How were these numbers worked out?

The recipe is the rice kheer in The Art & Science of Cooking (via the Indian Nutrient Databank): 10 g of rice cooked in half a cup of water and 250 ml of whole milk, with 1½ tablespoons of sugar, raisins and almond, for one bowl, at IFCT 2017 values with sugar from USDA. The databank counts all the milk and water as still there, a thin kheer; this page takes them as simmered down to about 270 g, so the bowl is about 300 g, an assumption, and 100 calories per 100 g. A katori is measured by volume at USDA's rice pudding density of 1.1 g per ml, so a 200 ml katori is about 220 g and 225 calories. USDA's analysed rice pudding, 108 calories per 100 g, agrees within 5%. No sweet-shop kheer has been analysed; the range uses the ratio of USDA's firni to its rice pudding, 1.33 times. Questions people ask

How many calories are in 1 katori of kheer?

About 225 for a 200 ml katori of home rice kheer made with whole milk, and about 170 for a small 150 ml katori.

How many calories are in a bowl of kheer?

About 305 for the textbook bowl, the whole recipe of 250 ml of milk, or about 280 for a big 250 ml katori.

How much sugar is in kheer?

About 14 g of added sugar in a 200 ml katori of this recipe, plus about 8 g of the milk's own sugar.

How many calories are in 100 g of kheer?

About 100. USDA's analysed rice pudding is 108 per 100 g.

Which has more calories, a katori of kheer or a gulab jamun?

A katori of kheer, about 225, against about 155 for one gulab jamun. Two gulab jamuns come to about 310.
