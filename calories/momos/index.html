Momos calories

By Nirmal Amin. Checked on 14 October 2026.

One steamed veg momo, about 25 g, has about 40 calories, so a plate of 6 is about 245 before the chutney or mayonnaise. A plate of 8 is about 325, and 100 g about 165. Frying changes it most, and mayonnaise adds about 100 calories a tablespoon. A street plate of 6 runs about 245 steamed to 345 fried.

Momos are dumplings of thin maida dough filled with cabbage, carrot, onion and ginger, or with minced chicken, then pleated and steamed. They came from Tibet and Nepal through Darjeeling and Sikkim, and are now sold at street carts across north India, steamed, fried or tandoori.

The maida wrapper is about four fifths of a veg momo's calories. Frying adds a lot; the red chutney adds little, and mayonnaise adds a lot.

Typical values for a home kitchen, for the portion named next to each number. Other sites often show bigger numbers because they count a bigger serving or a restaurant recipe. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

How does home momos compare with outside?

Street carts sell momos steamed, fried, or tossed in sauce. Frying adds a lot: USDA's fried dumplings have about three times the fat of steamed ones. Mayonnaise on the side adds about 100 calories a tablespoon.

What changes the calories in momos?

  • Fried or tandoori momos have much more fat than steamed ones.
  • Mayonnaise adds about 100 calories a tablespoon; the red chilli-garlic chutney adds very little.
  • Chicken momos are close per piece: USDA's steamed dumplings with meat are 113 calories per 100 g, against 125 without.
  • Paneer and cheese momos have more fat in the filling.

How were these numbers worked out?

No standard textbook in the Indian Nutrient Databank has momos, so Slate uses a published dietitian's recipe from NephroPlus: 200 g of maida with 100 g each of cabbage and carrot, 25 g of spring onion and spices, cooked in 3 teaspoons of oil, counted as eaten, at IFCT 2017 values. The recipe's water is not stated, so 100 ml is assumed for a stiff dough, bringing the batch to about 554 g, 165 calories per 100 g. Each momo is taken as 25 g, the size USDA lists for a dumpling, so a plate of 6 is 150 g and 245 calories. USDA's survey dumpling without meat, 125 calories per 100 g, is 23% below these thicker-wrapped momos, so the page shows a range. The street range covers frying: USDA's fried dumpling carries about 7.7 g more fat per 100 g than a steamed one, about 12 g of oil for a plate.
