Sambar calories

A 200 ml katori of thick sambar made with coconut has about 200 calories. A thinner everyday sambar without coconut is closer to 110, ICMR-NIN's figure for a cup.

Sambar is toor dal cooked with tamarind, sambar powder and vegetables such as drumstick, brinjal, okra and shallots, tempered with mustard seeds and curry leaves. It's eaten across south India with rice, idli, dosa and vada, and every state and family makes it differently.

This textbook sambar grinds roasted coconut into the masala, as in many Kerala and Udupi homes. The coconut is about a quarter of its calories, so whether yours has it matters.

Typical values for a home kitchen. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

Home or outside

Sambar from an Udupi restaurant or an office canteen is usually thinner than this textbook one and refilled freely, so the katori matters less than how many you have.

What changes the number

  • Coconut is the biggest single variable. The 30 g of fresh coconut here adds about 120 calories to the pot, about a quarter of the total.
  • Thickness decides how much dal is in a katori. A thin, everyday sambar has about half of this one per katori.
  • The tadka oil adds about 40 calories a teaspoon to the pot. Some homes finish with a spoon of ghee.
  • Vegetables add little: drumstick, brinjal, okra and tomato together are about 30 calories in the whole pot.

How these numbers were worked out

The recipe is the sambar in The Art & Science of Cooking, a standard Indian cookery textbook (via the Indian Nutrient Databank): 50 g toor dal with a teaspoon each of roasted chana and urad dal, 30 g fresh coconut, tamarind, drumstick, brinjal, okra, tomato and onion, cooked in a cup of water with 2 teaspoons of oil, for 2 bowls. Values are from IFCT 2017.

The cooked weight is the ingredients added together, and katoris are worked out at 1 g per ml.

That works out to about 100 calories per 100 g of cooked sambar. Calories are rounded to the nearest 5 and grams to the nearest whole number.

ICMR-NIN counts a cup of sambar at 110 calories, about half this page's 200. Most of the difference is thickness and the coconut. Depending on how it's made, a 200 ml katori of sambar can be anywhere from about 110 to 200.

Questions people ask

How many calories are in 1 cup of sambar?

About 110 for a thin everyday sambar, ICMR-NIN's figure, and about 200 for this thick sambar with coconut.

How many calories are in idli sambar?

Two medium idlis are about 105 calories. Add 110 to 200 for a 200 ml katori of sambar, depending on how thick it is.

How much protein is in sambar?

About 7 g in a 200 ml katori of this sambar, mostly from the toor dal.

How many calories are in 100 g of sambar?

About 100 for this thick sambar with coconut, and about 55 for a thin one. USDA's survey value is 86.

Which has more calories, sambar or rasam?

Sambar, by a lot. Rasam has very little dal and no coconut: a 200 ml katori of rasam is about 50 calories.
