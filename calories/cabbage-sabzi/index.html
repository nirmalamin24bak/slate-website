Cabbage sabzi calories

A 200 ml katori of cabbage and peas sabzi has about 85 calories. A whole textbook bowl, with 100 g of cabbage and a teaspoon of oil, is about 110.

Cabbage sabzi is shredded cabbage cooked quickly with peas, ginger, green chilli and turmeric, often with a mustard or jeera tempering. South Indian cabbage poriyal adds grated coconut and urad dal in the tadka, and Gujarati kobi nu shaak often has a little potato or capsicum.

Cabbage has only about 22 calories per 100 g, so peas and oil make up most of this sabzi's calories, in about equal parts.

Typical values for a home kitchen. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

What changes the number

  • Oil matters most. This recipe uses a single teaspoon; each extra teaspoon adds about 40 calories.
  • Grated coconut in poriyal adds about 40 calories per tablespoon (10 g).
  • Potato in place of peas changes it little; capsicum or carrot lowers it slightly.
  • Cabbage cooks down a lot, so a katori of cooked cabbage holds far more than it looks.

How these numbers were worked out

The recipe is the cabbage and peas in The Art & Science of Cooking, a standard Indian cookery textbook (via the Indian Nutrient Databank): 100 g cabbage and 50 g peas with ginger, green chilli and turmeric, cooked in a teaspoon of oil. Values are from IFCT 2017.

The cooked weight is the ingredients added together. Katoris use USDA's cooked shredded cabbage, 75 g per half cup.

That works out to about 65 calories per 100 g of cooked cabbage sabzi. Calories are rounded to the nearest 5 and grams to the nearest whole number.

ICMR-NIN's general figure for a cup of dry vegetable dish is 150 calories, far above this page's 85. NIN's figure covers all dry vegetables, including potato, with more oil. Cabbage sabzi made with 2 to 3 teaspoons of oil comes to about 115 to 145 a katori.
