Dal tadka calories

A 200 ml katori of thin toor dal tadka has about 115 calories. A thick dal can have close to twice that, because each katori holds more dal.

Dal tadka is toor (arhar) dal cooked soft and finished with a tadka: ghee heated with cumin, garlic, ginger and chilli, poured over at the end. The Gujarati version is thinner, often sweet and sour with jaggery and kokum. Punjabi dal tadka adds an onion and tomato masala and is usually thicker.

Two things decide the number in your katori: how much ghee went into the tadka, and how thick the dal is.

Typical values for a home kitchen. Your own katori, roti and oil change the number; Slate adjusts for them.

Home or outside

Restaurant and dhaba dal is usually thicker than everyday home dal, and it's often finished with a second spoon of ghee or butter at the table. Dal fry is cooked with its masala in ghee and tends to be richer again.

Published restaurant-style recipes use about as much ghee per gram of dal as this one, so the range below treats the difference as thickness: the same dal, 1.5 to 2 times as thick.

What changes the number

  • Most of a thin dal is water. A katori of thick dal can hold twice as much dal, and twice the calories.
  • Each teaspoon of ghee or oil in the tadka adds about 45 calories to the pot. A generous tadka is the biggest difference between one kitchen's dal and the next.
  • Gujarati dal often has a spoon or two of jaggery, which adds about 20 calories a teaspoon to the pot.
  • Onion, tomato and spices add a few calories a katori.
  • Toor, moong and masoor dal are within a few percent of each other per gram in IFCT 2017, so switching dal matters less than the ghee.

How these numbers were worked out

The recipe follows the tempered arhar dal in Basic Food Preparation, a standard Indian cookery manual (via the Indian Nutrient Databank), without its spinach: 30 g of toor dal cooked in 300 ml of water, with 10 g of ghee, ginger, garlic, green chilli and cumin. Values are from IFCT 2017.

The cooked weight is the ingredients added together, with nothing allowed for water boiled off, so this is a thin dal. Katoris are worked out at 1 g per ml.

That works out to about 55 calories per 100 g of cooked dal tadka. Calories are rounded to the nearest 5 and grams to the nearest whole number.

ICMR-NIN counts half a cup (100 ml) of plain dal at 100 calories, close to twice this page's 55 for the same volume. The difference is thickness: NIN's dal holds far more dal in each cup. Depending on how thick it is, a 200 ml katori of dal tadka can be anywhere from about 115 to about twice that.

Questions people ask

How many calories are in 1 katori of dal?

A 200 ml katori of thin dal tadka has about 115 calories. A thick dal can be close to double, because the katori holds more dal and less water.

How much protein is in a katori of dal?

A 200 ml katori of this thin dal has about 4 g of protein. A thicker dal has more, in line with the extra dal.

How many calories does a ghee tadka add?

About 45 calories for each teaspoon of ghee, spread across everyone who eats from the pot. In this recipe the 10 g of ghee adds about 90 calories to a bowl.

Which has more calories, dal fry or dal tadka?

Usually dal fry, because the dal is cooked into an onion and tomato masala in ghee and is thicker. The dal is the same; the fat and the thickness are what change.
