# BRAND.md: Slate

The brand rules for Slate, the calorie journal. Use this for every piece of copy and every decision about tone, in the app, on the website, in the App Store listing, in emails and in social posts. Visual rules live in DESIGN.md.

Owner: Sonal Systems Private Limited, Vadodara. Last updated 8 October 2026.

---

## 1. What Slate is

A calorie journal for iPhone, built for urban Indians aged 24 to 35. You type what you ate on a line, the way you'd say it. Slate puts a number next to it. Everything else stays out of the way.

**Positioning line:** A calorie journal that gets out of the way.

**The name.** A slate is a flat grey stone you write on. Chalk on, wipe off. It carries no opinion about what you wrote. It doesn't reward you for writing every day and it doesn't judge the number.

**Who it's for.** People in Indian cities who eat home food, tiffin, office canteen, street food and delivery in the same week, and who gave up on calorie apps built around bagels and Western portions.

**What it is not.** A coach, a nutritionist, a diet plan, a wellness platform, a social network or a habit app.

**The one-sentence test.** If a change makes Slate slower to open, slower to type into, or louder, it is wrong, however good the feature is.

## 2. What Slate stands for

| Principle | What it means |
|---|---|
| Instruments, not instructions | Slate shows numbers: BMR, baseline, measured maintenance. The user sets their own goal. Slate never suggests one, never nudges, never congratulates. A ruler, not a coach. |
| Honest about being an estimate | Labels can be 20% out. Restaurant oil can swing a dish by 40%. A number that is estimated the same way every time still tells the truth about your week. Every number shows how it was worked out. |
| The household, not the database | Other apps have more food entries. None of them know your katori, your roti, how long your oil lasts or how you take chai. Slate does. |
| Nothing between you and the line | No submit button, no forms, no search box, no modal, no celebration. |
| No login. No ads. No coaches. No sales calls. | Literal and permanent. |

## 4. Voice

Calm, factual, plain, warm without being chatty. Slate sounds like a sensible friend who happens to know food composition tables, not like a fitness instructor or a wellness brand.

| Slate is | Slate is not |
|---|---|
| Plain | Clever |
| Factual | Motivational |
| Quiet | Excited |
| Specific (katori, roti, Swiggy) | Generic (portion, meal, food item) |
| Respectful of the user's choices | Judgemental, praising or worried |
| Honest about limits | Promising results |

### 4.1 Rules

- Short sentences. Sentence case everywhere, including headings, buttons and tabs.
- **No exclamation marks.**
- **No em dashes** in any user-facing copy. Use a full stop, a comma or a colon.
- **No emoji.**
- **Never use:** just, healthy, journey, great job, cheat, cheat day, guilt-free, crush, smash, transform, burn fat, detox, superfood, clean eating, junk food, sinful, indulge, earn your food.
- Never praise eating less. Never criticise eating more. No "good" or "bad" foods.
- Never suggest a calorie goal, diet, meal plan, fast or target weight. Not in the app, not in chat, not in notifications, not on the website.
- Never comment on bodies, shape or appearance.
- Never promise results ("lose 5 kg", "get fit", "see results in 2 weeks").
- Over goal is stated plainly ("210 over") and never coloured, alarmed or commented on.
- No urgency or scarcity: no "limited time", "only today", countdowns or "don't miss out".
- Say "you" and "your". Slate refers to itself as "Slate", not "we", inside the app. "We" is used only where the company speaks (the promises, support emails, the website's about section, legal pages).

### 4.2 Indian context

- Use the words people use: katori, roti, chapati, dal, sabzi, poha, thali, tiffin, dabba, chai, chaas, Swiggy, Zomato, canteen, dhaba.
- Hinglish and Gujarati-English examples are welcome: "2 roti aur ek katori dal", "kam tel", "bina cheeni", "ghee wali", "half plate".
- Respect diet and practice without comment: vegetarian, eggetarian, Jain, fasting days, Ekadashi, Navratri. Never treat them as restrictions to work around.
- Numbers use Indian grouping with Western digits: 1,619 and ₹2,999. Rupees always with the ₹ sign.

### 4.3 Units and numbers

- "cals" next to numbers in the UI ("1,532 cals"). "calories" in sentences and in VoiceOver.
- Grams as "g" with a space: "21 g". Millilitres as "ml": "250 ml". Kilograms: "72 kg".
- Round displayed calories to whole numbers, and measured maintenance to the nearest 10.
- Exercise and steps are shown as negatives with a leading minus: "−250".

## 5. Copy patterns

### 5.1 The words we use for things

| Use | Don't use |
|---|---|
| Slate (the page you write on), line | Journal (inside the app), diary, log entry, food item |
| What did you have? | Log your meal, Add food |
| Your kitchen | Calibration, settings |
| How we got this | Nutrition breakdown, Details |
| Goal (the user's own number) | Target, budget, allowance |
| Left / over | Remaining / exceeded |
| Baseline | Maintenance calories (for the formula figure) |
| Measured maintenance | TDEE, true burn |
| Outside / Made at home | Restaurant / homemade |
| Slate Plus | Pro, Premium, Gold |
| No thanks | Maybe later, Not now, Skip |
| Delete | Remove forever |

### 5.2 Examples

| Situation | Write | Not |
|---|---|---|
| Empty journal | What did you have? | Start your day right |
| Over goal | 210 over | You've gone over your goal |
| Unrecognised line | ↻ (tap to try again) | Oops, we couldn't find that |
| Weight goes down | 78.4 kg | Great progress |
| Streak breaks | (nothing) | Don't lose your streak |
| Baseline moved | Your baseline has moved from 1,943 to 1,880 since 12 Sep. | Time to lower your goal |
| Measured maintenance | Based on what you've logged and your weight trend, your body has been using about 2,180 cals a day. | You should eat 1,680 to lose weight |
| Chat failed | Chat couldn't answer. Tap to try again. | Something went wrong |
| Under 18 | Slate isn't built for under-18s. | You're too young |
| Goal below 1,200 | Slate can't set a goal below 1,200 calories. | That's dangerous |
| Paywall headline | Slate is free forever. | Unlock your full potential |
| Photo teaser | Take a photo of your plate. Slate spots each dish, you confirm the portions. | Snap a food to get instant nutrition info |
| Data source | Estimated from IFCT 2017 values | Scientifically accurate |


### 5.3 Sensitive moments

- If someone describes not eating for long periods, purging, compensating with exercise or feeling out of control around food: respond warmly, give no numbers or tips, suggest talking to someone they trust or a professional, and offer Tele-MANAS (14416, free, any time). No claims about confidentiality.
- Medical questions: "Slate isn't medical advice. A doctor is the right person for this."
- Never diagnose, never name conditions the user didn't name.

## 6. Free and Slate Plus (how to talk about money)

- Lead with "Slate is free forever." Plus is described as optional, never as unlocking the "real" app.
- Free forever: the journal (food, exercise, weight, water, steps), Hinglish and Gujarati spellings, your kitchen, About you and How you cook, outside or home and portions, barcode and label scanning, macros and macro goals, How we got this, BMR and baseline, water, streak and 30 days of history, Siri, Hide calorie counts, export and delete, the promises.
- Slate Plus: photo logging, voice logging, delivery orders (share a Swiggy or Zomato order), chat, measured maintenance, stats and weight trend, Apple Health (Apple Watch, Garmin, Strava, Nike Run Club and more), widgets with quick add, custom dishes, saved foods, fiber and sugar, sleep, full history.
- Prices: ₹399 a month (about ₹92 a week) or ₹2,999 a year (about ₹58 a week, 37% less), 7 days free on yearly for eligible new subscribers.
- Always say how to cancel (iPhone Settings) and that nothing is deleted when Plus ends.
- Never discount loudly, never strike through prices, never use countdowns.

## 8. Imagery

Almost none. Slate is a text field.

- Where imagery is unavoidable (App Store screenshots, social posts), use real Indian food on real tables in daylight: a steel thali, a chipped katori, rotis under a cloth, a cutting chai glass. Never a styled grain bowl on marble.
- No bodies, no before and after, no measuring tapes, no scales, no gym imagery, no stock smiles.
- Line icons only, 1.5 px stroke, rounded caps.
- Never show other companies' logos (Apple Watch, Garmin, Strava, Nike). Name them in text only.

## 9. Distinctiveness (legal hygiene)

Slate shares an idea with other text-first calorie apps. It must look and sound like itself.

Never use:

- A ring or donut progress chart, ringed circular buttons, or a floating summary card pinned at the bottom.
- Two-chapter onboarding progress pills or a dots "consistency" graphic.
- Competitor copy, including: "as quickly as a notes app", "Hide calorie counts whenever you want", "Type anything, including exercise", "Want to be precise? Add detail", "Tap a date twice to change the start date", "No weight entries yet", "Snap a food to get instant nutrition info", "Try it out".
- Competitor sample numbers in screenshots or marketing: 790 / 2400, 1610 left, 32 g / 98 g / 27 g.
- Competitor names anywhere except the factual comparison pages on writeonslate.com/compare/. There, every claim is sourced and dated, there are no logos or screenshots, nothing disparaging, and each page says honestly where the other app is a better fit. Never name Mist.
