# Food sources and arithmetic

Generated by `node scripts/build.mjs` from data/foods.json and data/ingredients.json. Do not edit by hand: change the data and rebuild.

Energy per ingredient is IFCT 2017's own value (kJ converted at 4.184 kJ per kcal). Pure fats (oils, ghee) have no energy value in our IFCT copy, so they use IFCT's fat factor of 37 kJ per g (about 8.84 kcal per g). Ingredients not in IFCT use USDA FoodData Central or the maker's label, as listed.

Cooked yield 133 g (atta x (1 - 0.111) / (1 - 0.33): IFCT's atta is 11.1% water and USDA's cooked chapati is 33% water). Per 100 g cooked: 240.8 kcal.

Portions:

Outside (Dhaba or restaurant, with ghee): fat sources x 1 to 1, portion x 1 to 1, plus 5 to 10 g Ghee = 140 to 185 kcal. Basis: No analysed data was found for restaurant or tandoori rotis, so this is a stated assumption: the same roti brushed with 1 to 2 NIN teaspoons (5 ml each) of ghee. Butter has slightly less fat than ghee, so butter rotis sit at or below this range.

Cross-checks:

- ICMR-NIN, Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2011), Annexure 8: phulka: 80 kcal per a phulka, ours 77 (+4%).  https://nin.res.in/downloads/DietaryGuidelinesforNINwebsite.pdf

Judgement calls:

- Medium roti = 30 g atta: NIN's cereal portion is 30 g raw (100 kcal). NIN's phulka at 80 kcal works out to about 24 g of atta. Large = 40 g is a judgement for bigger Punjabi-style rotis.
- The textbook chapati (The Art & Science of Cooking via INDB, ASC096) is 80 g atta and 1 tsp ghee for 4 chapatis: 20 g atta each, 72.8 kcal with its share of ghee. INDB's per-100 g value (202 kcal) is not used as a cross-check because INDB doesn't allow for water lost in cooking, so it is per 100 g of dough, not of cooked roti.
- Cooked yield 1.33 is derived from water contents (IFCT atta 11.1%, USDA chapati FDC 171844 33%). No Tier 1-3 source weighed a home roti directly.
- USDA's commercial chapatis (FDC 171844, 297 kcal per 100 g; FDC 174075, 299 kcal) are not used for values: they are factory-made and contain added fat.
- Teaspoon = 5 ml (NIN); 5 g of ghee taken as one teaspoon, matching NIN's 5 g fat portion of 45 kcal.
