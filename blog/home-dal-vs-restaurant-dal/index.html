Home dal vs restaurant dal: where the extra calories come from

By Nirmal Amin, Sonal Systems. Published 11 October 2026.

A katori of dal from a restaurant often has twice the calories of the same katori at home, sometimes more. The dal itself is much the same. The difference is how thick it is, how much ghee, butter and cream go in, and how big the serving is.

Key takeaways

  • A 200 ml katori of thin home dal tadka has about 115 calories; a thicker restaurant dal tadka about 170 to 225.
  • Thickness is the biggest hidden factor: a thick dal holds more dal and less water in every katori.
  • Each teaspoon of ghee or butter is about 35 to 45 calories, and restaurants often finish dal with more than one.
  • Dal makhani is a different dish altogether: about 200 for a home katori and 265 to 335 at a restaurant.

How much is in a katori of home dal

Start with what's in an everyday home dal. A textbook dal tadka from Basic Food Preparation, a standard Indian cookery manual, uses 30 g of toor dal in 300 ml of water, with 10 g of ghee between the dal and the tadka. A 200 ml katori of it has about 115 calories and 4 g of protein.

Most of the calories in that katori come from two places in roughly equal parts: the dal and the ghee. That's useful to know, because they're also the two things a restaurant changes.

Thickness: the difference you can't see on a menu

Home dal is often thin, poured over rice. Restaurant and dhaba dal is usually thicker, cooked down so it sits on a spoon. A thicker dal holds more dal and less water in every katori, so the same katori carries more calories even before any extra ghee.

When we compared a published restaurant-style dal tadka recipe with the textbook one, the fat per gram of dal was about the same. The difference per katori came mainly from thickness: restaurant dal at 1.5 to 2 times as thick gives about 170 to 225 calories in a 200 ml katori.

Dal fry shows the same thing at home. With its onion-tomato masala cooked in and half the water, a 200 ml katori is about 180 calories, against about 115 for thin dal tadka.

Ghee, butter and the second tadka

Fat is the other half of the story. A teaspoon of ghee is about 45 calories and a teaspoon of butter about 35, from IFCT 2017 and USDA values. Restaurants often add a second tadka right before serving, or a spoon of butter on top, and a dhaba dal fry may have its masala fried in more ghee than a home kitchen would use.

You can sometimes see it: a shine of ghee on top, a pool of butter melting in the centre. When you can't see it, assume there's more fat than at home rather than less.

Dal makhani: butter and cream

Dal makhani is the richest dal on most menus. It's whole urad with a little rajma, simmered for hours and finished with butter and cream. A textbook home version, the Moti Mahal dal in The Art & Science of Cooking, comes to about 200 calories for a 200 ml katori.

Restaurant versions use much more of both. An open recipe in the Indian Nutrient Databank uses 3 tablespoons of butter and 2.5 of cream for 180 g of dal. A restaurant katori, finished with a teaspoon or two of butter and a swirl of cream, is about 265 to 335 calories.

The serving itself

A restaurant serving of dal isn't one katori by definition. Bowls and delivery containers vary, and many are meant for sharing. Counting what you actually ate, in your own katoris, matters as much as knowing the calories per katori.

How to count dal when you eat out

  1. Count by katori, not by order. Estimate how many of your katoris the serving holds.
  2. Use the restaurant range, not the home number. The middle of the range is a fair default.
  3. Add visible fat: a spoon of butter or ghee on top is another 35 to 45 calories.
  4. Treat dal makhani as its own dish, not as dal.
  5. If you share, count your share.

None of these numbers is exact. A restaurant doesn't publish its recipe, and two dhabas on the same road will make different dal. The aim is a fair estimate you can repeat, not a precise one.

Ordering dal with less fat

If you'd like restaurant dal closer to home dal, the levers are the same ones that raise it. Plain yellow dal or dal tadka usually carries less fat than dal fry, and dal fry less than dal makhani. Many restaurants will leave off the butter on top or the cream if you ask, and a second tadka is easy to skip. Each teaspoon of ghee or butter you leave out is about 35 to 45 calories.

Questions people ask

Why does restaurant dal have more calories than home dal?

Restaurant dal is usually thicker, finished with more ghee, butter or cream, and served in bigger portions. The dal itself is much the same.

How many calories are in a katori of restaurant dal tadka?

About 170 to 225 for a 200 ml katori, against about 115 for thin home dal tadka.

How many calories are in restaurant dal makhani?

About 265 to 335 for a 200 ml katori, depending on the butter and cream.
