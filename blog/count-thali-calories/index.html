How to count calories in a thali without weighing anything

By Nirmal Amin, Sonal Systems. Published 11 October 2026.

You don't need a scale to count a thali. Count the pieces (rotis, puris, papad, sweets), count the katoris (dal, sabzi, kadhi, rice), and add the ghee you can see. That gets you a fair estimate.

Key takeaways

  • Break the thali into pieces and katoris, and count each one.
  • Rotis and rice are usually the biggest part of the total; sweets and fried farsan add the most per bite.
  • Count every refill as another katori or another roti.
  • Ghee poured at the table is about 45 calories a teaspoon and easy to miss.

The method: pieces and katoris

A thali looks complicated because it has many small things on it. Counted one by one, it's simple:

  1. Pieces: rotis, puris, theplas, papad, farsan, sweets. Count how many.
  2. Katoris: dal, kadhi, sabzi, raita, kheer. Count how many, and how full.
  3. Rice: count it in katoris too. A 200 ml katori of plain rice is about 160 calories.
  4. Fat you can see: ghee on the rice or dal, butter on a roti. About 45 calories a teaspoon for ghee.
  5. Refills: every extra roti or katori is another one.

If the thali's katoris are smaller or bigger than yours at home, count them as a fraction of yours.

A Gujarati thali

A Gujarati thali usually has rotlis, dal, bhaat, a shaak, kadhi, a farsan such as dhokla, and a sweet. As home values:

A Punjabi thali

A Punjabi thali often has roti or naan, dal makhani, a paneer dish, rice and raita. Made at home it comes to about this; restaurant versions carry more butter and cream, so use the restaurant ranges on each page if you ate out:

Home values. A restaurant thali is usually well above this.

Swap the rotis for one large restaurant butter naan, about 595, and the total rises by about 400 calories on its own.

A South Indian meal

A South Indian meals plate is rice-based: rice with sambar, rasam, a poriyal, curd and papad, often with refills of rice and sambar. As home values:

Home values. Sambar varies a lot with thickness and coconut.
ItemAbout
Rice, two 200 ml katoris320
Thick sambar with coconut, 150 ml katori150
Rasam, 150 ml katori40
Cabbage sabzi (poriyal without coconut), 150 ml katori65
Total, before curd and papadabout 570

Rice is most of it, so the rice refills are the thing to count carefully here. A thinner everyday sambar without coconut has about half the calories per katori.

Home thali or restaurant thali

The same thali from a restaurant is usually higher, for the same reasons restaurant food usually is: more ghee, butter and cream, and refills that keep coming. In a restaurant, a 200 ml katori of dal makhani is about 265 to 335 calories, against about 200 at home, and a restaurant serving of paneer butter masala is about 390 to 725, against about 230 for a home bowl.

Wedding and festival thalis add more fried food and more sweets, and a buffet makes the portions harder to see. Counting pieces and katoris works for those too.

Chutneys, pickle and papad

The small things on the side are easy to skip, and some of them add up. ICMR-NIN's Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2011) count 2 tablespoons of coconut, groundnut or til chutney at about 120 calories and a tablespoon of tamarind chutney with jaggery at about 60, while a tablespoon of tomato chutney is about 10. Pickles in oil carry the oil, and a fried papad carries the oil it was fried in.

Where thali calories usually hide

  • Ghee on the rice and dal, poured at the table.
  • Fried farsan: a home samosa is about 165 calories, a kachori about 120.
  • Puris instead of rotis: four home puris are about 385.
  • Sweets: a gulab jamun is about 155, a piece of kaju katli about 50.
  • Refills of rice and roti, which are easy to stop counting.

How exact this needs to be

A thali counted this way gives an estimate. Most restaurants don't publish their recipes, and every home cooks differently, so counting the same way each time is what makes your numbers comparable from one week to the next.

In Slate you can write a thali as one line, "4 rotli, dal, bhaat, shaak, kadhi", and each item gets its own line and its own number, using your katori and roti sizes for home food.

Questions people ask

How many calories are in a Gujarati thali?

About 1,040 for a home thali with 4 small rotlis, dal, rice, sabzi, kadhi, 2 pieces of dhokla and a gulab jamun. Restaurant thalis with refills and ghee are usually more.

Can I count a thali without weighing?

Yes. Count the pieces and the katoris, add the ghee you can see, and count every refill.

What's the highest-calorie part of a thali?

Usually whatever you eat most of: the rotis, the rice or a rich curry such as paneer butter masala. Per bite, sweets and fried farsan add the most.

How many calories are in a South Indian meals plate?

About 570 for two 200 ml katoris of rice with sambar, rasam and poriyal at home, before curd, papad and refills.

How much does ghee on rice add?

About 45 calories for each teaspoon. Two spoons poured over rice and dal at the table is about 90.
