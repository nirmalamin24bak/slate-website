Your kitchen, not a database.

Every other app has more food entries. None of them know how big your katori is, or how long a litre of oil lasts your family. Slate asks four questions, once.

  • Your katori.

    150, 200 or 250 ml.

  • Your roti.

    6, 8 or 10 inches.

  • Your oil.

    "A litre lasts us 30 days, for 4 people."

  • Your chai.

    Sugar and milk, for chai and coffee.

Home or outside.

Restaurant and delivery food carries more oil and bigger portions than home food. Slate counts them differently, and you can switch any line with one tap.

About you.

Tell Slate how you eat: vegetarian or Jain, fasting days, tiffin or canteen, how you cook. It reads your lines the way you mean them.

All of this is free, for everyone.

Every number here is an estimate.

Packet labels can be 20% out. Restaurant oil can be more. Slate is built to be consistent, so your week tells the truth even when a single day is rough.

Numbers are worked out from Indian food composition data and adjusted for your kitchen. AI only reads what you wrote. It never makes up a calorie.

Instruments, not instructions.

Slate shows you the numbers. You decide what to do with them.

  • Your baseline.

    Your BMR and the calories you'd use on a quiet day, worked out from your body. They update as your weight trend moves, and Slate tells you when they've changed.

  • Your goal.

    One number you type yourself. Slate never suggests one, and never praises you for eating less.

  • Measured maintenance Plus

    After about three weeks of logging and a few weigh-ins, Slate works out what your body has actually been using each day.

Things people ask

Is Slate really free?

Yes. The journal, your kitchen, scanning, macros and How we got this are free forever, and we've promised never to move a free feature behind Plus. Slate Plus adds photo logging, chat, stats, measured maintenance, Apple Health, widgets and more.

Do I need an account?

No. There's no login, email or phone number. Slate creates an anonymous account on your phone.

Where is my data stored?

In Mumbai, India. Nobody else can see your journal, and you can export or delete all of it from Settings.

How accurate are the numbers?

They're estimates, like every calorie number. Slate uses Indian food composition data and adjusts for your katori, roti, oil and chai, so the numbers are consistent. Tap any number to see how it was worked out.

Does Slate use AI?

Only to read lines it doesn't recognise, to spot dishes in a photo, and for chat. The AI never produces a calorie number. Your journal isn't used to train AI models.

Will Slate tell me what my goal should be?

No. Slate shows your BMR, your baseline and, with Plus, your measured maintenance. The goal is yours to set.

Does it work with Apple Watch, Garmin or Strava?

Yes, with Slate Plus, through Apple Health. If an app writes to Apple Health, Slate can read it.

Can I write in Hinglish or Gujarati?

Yes. "2 roti aur ek katori dal", "kam tel", "bina cheeni" and Gujarati spellings all work. The app itself is in English for now.

Is there an Android version?

Not yet. Slate is on iPhone first.

How do I cancel Slate Plus?

In your iPhone's Settings, under your name, then Subscriptions. Cancel at least 24 hours before it renews. Nothing in your journal is deleted.

Who can use Slate?

Anyone 18 or older. Slate isn't built for under-18s.

Is Slate medical advice?

No. If you have a medical condition, or your relationship with food feels hard, talk to a doctor. In India, Tele-MANAS is free and available any time on 14416.
